Publications from the project will be listed here as they appear, with preprints and data linked where available. The work below is the foundation the project starts from.

Foundational technologies

CEDAR Stanford University

The Center for Expanded Data Annotation and Retrieval. An ontology-aware, template-based editing system for authoring structured metadata that enforces data types, units, and ontology terms. OPAL templates are implemented in its Workbench.

BioPortal Stanford University

The world’s largest repository of biomedical ontologies, which define domain terms and their relationships to support consistent interpretation of data.

Protégé Stanford University

The most widely used technology for building and managing terminologies and ontologies.

SciBORG Purdue University

Scientific Bespoke Artificial Intelligence Agents Optimized for Research Goals: a finite-state agent framework for reliable, auditable control of complex laboratory workflows.

CLAW Purdue University

Comprehensive Lipidomic Automation Workflow: LLM-guided LC–MS pipelines with embedded quality control, system suitability checks, and statistical validation.

Symbolic Lab Language Emerald Cloud Lab

The execution language that drives experiments at Emerald, contributed to the PCL Test Bed under an open-source license and the first target for TROVE translation. A link to the open-source repository will be added when it is public.

Frameworks under review

OPAL’s workflow layer is being designed against existing approaches to encoding laboratory procedures, including LabOP, Autoprotocol, BioCoder, Aquarium, and XDL, alongside mature computational workflow frameworks such as the Common Workflow Language and Nextflow. OPAL’s expressiveness will be benchmarked against community protocols curated on protocols.io.
