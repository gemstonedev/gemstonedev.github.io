  The GEMSTONE team joined the PCLNet Summit in Washington, D.C., on
  September 24–25, 2026, to share its vision for laboratory automation and
  explore how common workflow standards can connect the network's
  programmable cloud laboratories.
---

The GEMSTONE team joined researchers from across the National Science
Foundation's Programmable Cloud Laboratories network at the
[PCLNet Summit](https://ebrc.org/national-network-of-ai-programmable-cloud-laboratories-summit-pcl-kickoff/)
in Washington, D.C., on September 24&ndash;25, 2026. The meeting brought
together the network's 20 funded teams to share their plans and explore
opportunities to work together.

Convened by NSF, the Astera Institute, and the Engineering Biology Research
Consortium, the two-day summit combined project presentations, posters, and
discussions on the foundations of a connected laboratory network. Topics
included AI, shared infrastructure, data standards, and researcher access to
cloud laboratories.

GEMSTONE's presentation and [poster]({{ '/publications/' | relative_url }})
highlighted the project's work toward translating written experimental
protocols into instructions that automated laboratories can execute. At the
center of this effort is AMBER, a shared workflow representation being
developed to describe experiments independently of any one laboratory's
equipment or software. Together with AI tools for interpreting protocols and
generating laboratory-specific instructions, it aims to make experimental
methods easier to share and reuse.

The summit also provided an opportunity to discuss how these ideas could
support collaboration across PCLNet. GEMSTONE is exploring common workflow
representations that allow laboratories to exchange experimental methods
while retaining their own execution systems. Follow-up discussions with
other teams are helping identify candidate workflows and scientific
questions for collaboration.
